# Week 2: Condensed

## Working thesis

Self-awareness, self-regulation, and epistemic rigor are important for using AI in ways that support personal well-being, effective action, and societal flourishing. We need to stay connected to our own experience, evaluate what AI gives us, and retain responsibility for our judgment and decisions.

## Three categories of failure

### 1. Personal well-being and relationships

- Worse mental health and relationships, including concerns about AI reinforcing delusional beliefs.
- Loss of self-connection: avoiding uncomfortable feelings, needs, and thoughts rather than becoming aware of them.
- Loss of self-trust and self-leadership: offloading judgment and responsibility for one's life to AI.

### 2. Productivity, learning, and outcomes

- Poor decisions from accepting AI responses without reviewing or questioning them.
- Loss of critical thinking and independent skill through avoiding the discomfort of not knowing and learning.
- Reinforcement of personal patterns, such as perfectionism and overthinking.
- Endless theorizing and exploring edge cases that feels productive but delays action and meeting reality.

### 3. Societal stability and flourishing

- Erosion of epistemic rigor: less attention to evidence, justification, and what warrants belief.
- Loss of independent judgment and critical thinking across society.
- Greater vulnerability to manipulation, propaganda, and reinforcement of unsupported beliefs.
